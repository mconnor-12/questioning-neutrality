— five findings from the research

01
August Problem

Students returning in August receive a harder exam than they sat in June

Schema burden averages 45.4 in August vs. 37.6 in June. Students below 55 average 35.1% in August vs. 10.7% in June. These are students completing a graduation requirement — and the instrument meets them with greater demand.

02
Skill Gap

The exam rewards source extraction far more reliably than historical context and causation

Across 5 USH sittings, the gap between binary CR item success and extended CRQ performance averages 24.9 percentage points. Reading what a document says is not the same skill as knowing why it was written.

03
Centering

US History centers dominant racial and national frames more often than Global II

USH items more often center white, national, elite, and state-sanctioned perspectives. Global II distributes attention more broadly across class, labor, coloniality, gender, religion, and race.

04
Transition

The GHG→USH transition has an 8.3-point standard deviation — the exams measure different things

Among 93 students in the primary cohort transition, mean scores rose +1.8 points — but with 8.3-point SD. Seven students dropped more than 10 points. The same preparation that succeeds in GHG does not reliably transfer to USH.

05
Hidden Pattern

Resistant voices score higher on neutrality challenge — and are perceived as less neutral

Dominant and institutional sources score 2.43–2.75 on the neutrality challenge scale. Resistant and community voices score 4.34. The sources that name power are read as biased. The sources that exercise it are read as neutral.

06
Root Cause

The exam doesn't just disadvantage students by being hard — it rewards the wrong answer

Thorough formation in the dominant cultural gaze — what the curriculum trains students to do — leads reliably to wrong answers on the most demanding items. This is not a design flaw. It is a structural feature.
